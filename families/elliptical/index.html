    Elliptical families¶

    An elliptical copula is the dependence structure left over when the margins of an elliptically contoured distribution are transformed to uniform. If \(F_\rho\) is the bivariate distribution function with correlation \(\rho\) and \(F\) is its univariate margin, then

    \[ C(u, v) = F_\rho\big(F^{-1}(u),\, F^{-1}(v)\big). \]

    Both elliptical families in fortvine are exchangeable and radially symmetric: the lower and upper tails behave alike, and the 180° rotation gives back the same copula. Negative dependence needs no rotation, because \(\rho\) can be negative. fortvine therefore registers no rotations for these two families.

    Throughout, \(x = F^{-1}(u)\) and \(y = F^{-1}(v)\) are the scores of the two arguments, and \(h_1(v \mid u) = \partial C / \partial u\) is the conditional distribution of \(V\) given \(U = u\).

    Density contours of the copula with standard normal margins. Left and middle: the same Kendall's \(\tau = 0.5\); the Student t puts more mass in both corners. Right: a Student t copula with \(\rho = 0\) is uncorrelated but not independent.

    Gaussian¶

    Property Value
    Code 1 (FV_GAUSSIAN)
    Parameter \(\rho\) (correlation)
    Box in fortvine \([-1, 1]\)
    Kendall's \(\tau\) \(\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\rho\), any value in \((-1, 1)\)
    \(\lambda_L\), \(\lambda_U\) \(0\), \(0\) (for \(\lvert\rho\rvert < 1\))
    Exchangeable yes
    Rotations none (radially symmetric; \(\rho < 0\) gives negative dependence)

    With \(\Phi\) the standard normal distribution function, \(x = \Phi^{-1}(u)\), \(y = \Phi^{-1}(v)\):

    \[ C(u, v) = \Phi_2(x, y; \rho), \qquad c(u, v) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}} \exp\!\left( -\frac{\rho^2 (x^2 + y^2) - 2 \rho x y}{2 (1 - \rho^2)} \right). \]

    The h-function and its inverse are both closed:

    \[ h_1(v \mid u) = \Phi\!\left( \frac{y - \rho x}{\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}} \right), \qquad h_1^{-1}(p \mid u) = \Phi\!\left( \rho x + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\, \Phi^{-1}(p) \right), \]

    and \(h_2\) is the same expression with the roles of \(x\) and \(y\) exchanged.

    Dependence measures. Kendall's \(\tau = \frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\rho\) and Spearman's \(\rho_S = \frac{6}{\pi}\arcsin(\rho/2)\). fortvine uses the first relation to start the fit (\(\rho = \sin(\pi\tau/2)\)) and for the FV_ITAU method.

    Notes.

    • The Gaussian copula is asymptotically independent in both tails: however strong \(\rho\) is, \(\lambda_L = \lambda_U = 0\) (Sibuya 1960). Joint extremes still occur more often than under independence, but at a rate that vanishes in the limit.
    • fortvine evaluates \(C\) itself (needed for discrete margins) by Gauss–Legendre quadrature of the exact h-function, since the bivariate normal integral has no elementary form.
    • At \(\rho = \pm 1\) the copula is singular (the comonotone or countermonotone copula); the box boundary is never reached in practice because the likelihood diverges there.

    Student t¶

    Property Value
    Code 2 (FV_STUDENT)
    Parameters \(\rho\) (correlation), \(\nu\) (degrees of freedom), in this order
    Box in fortvine \(\rho \in [-1, 1]\), \(\nu \in [2, 30]\)
    Kendall's \(\tau\) \(\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\rho\), independent of \(\nu\)
    \(\lambda_L = \lambda_U\) \(2\, T_{\nu+1}\!\left(-\sqrt{(\nu+1)\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}}\right)\)
    Exchangeable yes
    Rotations none (radially symmetric)

    Let \(t_\nu\) and \(T_\nu\) be the density and distribution function of the univariate Student t with \(\nu\) degrees of freedom, and \(x = T_\nu^{-1}(u)\), \(y = T_\nu^{-1}(v)\). The density is the bivariate t density divided by its two margins:

    \[ c(u, v) = \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+2}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)} {\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)^2 \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}} \; \frac{\left(1 + \dfrac{x^2 - 2\rho x y + y^2}{\nu (1 - \rho^2)}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+2}{2}}} {\left(1 + \dfrac{x^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}} \left(1 + \dfrac{y^2}{\nu}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}}. \]

    Given \(U = u\), the variable \(V\) is again of Student type, now with \(\nu + 1\) degrees of freedom and a scale that depends on \(x\). Hence the closed forms

    \[ h_1(v \mid u) = T_{\nu+1}\!\left( \frac{y - \rho x}{\sqrt{\dfrac{(\nu + x^2)(1 - \rho^2)}{\nu + 1}}} \right), \qquad h_1^{-1}(p \mid u) = T_\nu\!\left( \rho x + T_{\nu+1}^{-1}(p) \sqrt{\dfrac{(\nu + x^2)(1 - \rho^2)}{\nu + 1}} \right). \]

    Tail dependence. Both tail coefficients equal \(2\,T_{\nu+1}\big(-\sqrt{(\nu+1)(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)}\big)\) (Embrechts, McNeil & Straumann 2002; Demarta & McNeil 2005). They grow as \(\nu\) falls and as \(\rho\) rises, and they are positive even at \(\rho = 0\) and at moderately negative \(\rho\): a small \(\nu\) makes large values of both variables (of either sign) occur together, which is visible as the four "arms" in the right panel of the figure above.

    Notes.

    • Kendall's \(\tau\) does not involve \(\nu\), so \(\rho\) is started from \(\tau\) exactly as for the Gaussian. Under maximum likelihood, \((\rho, \nu)\) are then fitted jointly with \(\rho\) kept in the window \(\sin(\pi(\tau \pm 0.1)/2)\) (the practice of vinecopulib); under FV_ITAU, \(\rho\) stays at \(\sin(\pi\tau/2)\) and only \(\nu\) is estimated.
    • As \(\nu \to \infty\) the family tends to the Gaussian copula. The upper box limit \(\nu = 30\) is already close to that limit; selection then prefers the Gaussian because it has one parameter fewer.
    • The t extreme-value copula (code 23), the max-stable limit of the t copula, is listed with the extreme-value families.
    • Skew-t and other radially asymmetric elliptical constructions are not implemented; see families not implemented.
